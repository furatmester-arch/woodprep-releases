How to Add Edge Banding Data to Parts in AutoCAD

Recording what goes on each of the four edges — and keeping it attached to the part all the way to the saw.

Edge banding is not something you can read off a 3D model. The solid knows its size; it does not know that three edges get 0.8 mm and the visible front edge gets 2 mm ABS. That decision has to be recorded somewhere — and for most workshops that somewhere is a handwritten note next to the cutting list.

Why banding gets lost between design and production

The cutting list is usually generated first, and the banding is added afterwards from memory or from a marked-up printout. Two things go wrong with that:

  • The person adding the banding is often not the person who designed the cabinet
  • Nothing links the note back to the part — if the size changes, the banding note stays as it was

The result is the classic workshop problem: a door that comes back from the edgebander with the wrong edge finished, on a part that was cut correctly.

Record it per edge, while you go through the parts

In WoodPrep the banding is entered at the moment each part is processed, not afterwards. Every part has four edges, and each edge can get its own material and thickness — you can mix freely on the same part.

The same dialog also handles grain direction: you can lock it, so the part must be cut with the grain running a fixed way, or allow it to rotate, giving the cutting optimization freedom to save material. On a part where the grain does not matter, letting it rotate is often the difference between one board and two.

Two identical parts: one banded 2 mm ABS all round, the other with four different edges — and the grain direction switched on the second. Hover to play, click to enlarge.

Step by step

Run PP26 on the part

The size is read from the solid. The dialog then asks for the production data the geometry cannot supply.

Set each edge separately

Assign material and thickness edge by edge. Same material all round, or four different ones — whatever the part actually needs.

Decide about the grain

Lock the direction where it is visible, or let it rotate where it is not, so the optimization can nest more tightly.

Export to Excel

The banding travels with the part into the cutting list — it is a column of the sheet, not a note beside it.

What you end up with

  • Banding recorded per edge, on the part it belongs to
  • Different materials and thicknesses on the same part where needed
  • Grain direction locked or free, decided per part
  • Nothing to transcribe later — it is already in the Excel sheet
